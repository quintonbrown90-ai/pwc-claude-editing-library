## The character [MEASURED]

- One recurring cartoon man, identical in every video: round face, light tan
  skin, dark brown/black hair, full beard + moustache, black round glasses,
  beige/tan crew-neck shirt. Flat vector illustration, soft cel shading,
  thin darker outline. He is a *presenter puppet*, not a mascot on the side.
- Anchor position: **bottom-center**, occupying roughly the bottom 22–28% of
  frame height, waist-up. The stage above him is where evidence appears.
- He **reacts on the beat**: a new pose roughly every 1–3 seconds, matched to
  the spoken content — holding a phone while "everyone's making reels,"
  shrugging at "nobody's showing you how," pointing up at a claim, hand on
  chin while a problem is stated, holding the case file, peering through a
  magnifying glass during self-review, waving at the CTA. Poses are a
  *library*, reused across videos.
- When a scene has no document to show, his pose carries the beat (think,
  point, shrug, celebrate). He never leaves the frame for long; the viewer
  always has the same "host" to read emotion from.

## The stage: dossier / case-file world [MEASURED — the hero's costume]

In the hero, every scene is a document on a desk, photographed top-down:

- **Backgrounds:** kraft/tan paper texture (warm mid-brown, ≈ #B98D5F–#C9A36A)
  for evidence scenes; dark wood desk band along the bottom edge; cream file
  paper (≈ #F4EBD8) for the documents themselves; a **wall of his own reel
  thumbnails** as wallpaper for meta scenes ("most AI reels," "every reel on
  this account").
- **The document is the hero object.** One center-stage card per beat,
  occupying the middle 55–70% of frame height: a SUBJECT FILE, a calendar
  card, a pyramid diagram, a terminal window, a film strip. It enters with
  motion (see MOTION.md), does its one job, and is replaced.
- **Recurring document types** (his component library):
  - *Subject file card* — photo window (silhouette or avatar), typed fields,
    red stamped values; used for stats and identity claims.
  - *Stamp* — big rotated grunge text (SLOP, FIXED, SEALED, NO PHOTO ON FILE,
    CLOSING FAST), almost always stamp-red (≈ #C23B2A). The stamp is his
    verdict device: a claim lands, the stamp judges it.
  - *Exhibit card* — labeled evidence plate (EXHIBIT A/B/C/D + title +
    diagram). The hero reel is literally structured as four exhibits.
  - *Diagram card* — pyramid (competition), curve graph (spring overshoot),
    gauge (TUNED), timeline ruler (caption timing), tape-machine (audio
    chain). Diagrams are drawn simply: black ink lines, one red accent.
  - *Terminal card* — black Claude Code window, mono type, checkmarks.
  - *Film strip* — a row of reel thumbnails with a magnifier, for
    self-review / "decoded from my viral reels."
  - *Count-up card* — a number that ticks up in stamp-red display type
    (2,457 → 11,780 → 50,000 followers; 22 → 112 → 183 → 191 files, files
    fanning out like a hand of cards behind it).
- **Annotation layer [MEASURED]:** hand-drawn red marker circles, arrows and
  underlines appear on top of documents to point at the one thing that
  matters this beat ("FIRST" circled on the crowd, GAP arrows). Cheap to
  make, reads as *a human reviewing evidence* — a big part of why it doesn't
  feel like AI slop.

## Palette + type [MEASURED]

- Palette is a **two-ink system**: warm paper neutrals (kraft, cream, wood
  brown, ink black ≈ #1E1A16) + **one accent: stamp red**. No gradients, no
  neon, no second accent color. The discipline is the brand.
- Type: condensed display caps for headlines/stamps (heavy, slightly
  distressed on stamps), typewriter/mono for file labels and terminal text,
  small clean sans for body labels. Numbers in the count-ups use the stamp
  display face in red.
- Lighting/shading: flat with soft drop shadows under documents (paper on a
  desk), subtle paper grain. Nothing is 3D-rendered except device mockups
  (phones showing reels) which stay simple.

## Scene grammar [MEASURED, hero]

1. Establish: wall/grid of reels or a document slams in over the paper bg.
2. Evidence: center document shows the claim's proof (stat, diagram, file).
3. Verdict: a stamp, red circle, or the avatar's reaction closes the beat.
4. The top-of-frame caption strip runs word-by-word the whole time
   (see CAPTIONS.md), and the avatar emotes underneath it all.
