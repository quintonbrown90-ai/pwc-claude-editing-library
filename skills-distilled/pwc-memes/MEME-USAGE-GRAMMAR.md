## 1. What memes ARE in Q's system

Memes are one of Q's **signature devices** — his words, Nov 12, 2026: like the little-head clusters he took from Sky, meme placement done right becomes *his thing*. A meme is an emotion made visible: it says what the viewer's face is doing (shock, disbelief, laughing, "say less", celebration) at the exact moment they feel it. It is **seasoning and punctuation — never the meal, never proof.**

Q's Nov 12 expansion of the Nov 6 pack: memes may now be used as
- **(a) little pop-ups** while he keeps talking (the Nov 6 default),
- **(b) full-frame transition memes** — a meme takes the whole frame for a beat right after he says something, then the video returns to him,
- **(c) reaction / emotion beats after a line** — the meme reacts *for* the viewer immediately after a claim, a number, a mistake, a win.

Trigger language (no code words needed): "add memes", "put memes in", "add memes where appropriate in this video", "meme pop-ups", "reaction memes", "GIF inserts", "funny cutaways".

## 2. THE CLEAN-ONLY LAW (absolute, outranks everything)

- **No cursing anywhere in any banked or used meme asset** — not in its audio, not in burned-in text, not in the source moment. Q's content is clean and faith-aligned.
- Every meme passes the screening pipeline BEFORE it enters the bank and before it is used in an edit: download → faster-whisper transcription → profanity scan of the transcript → verdict (CLEAN / clean segments only / PARKED). Unscreened = unusable. No exceptions, no "it's just one word" — a meme with profanity is cut around or parked, never shipped.
- Also faith-clean beyond profanity: no vulgar, mean-spirited, or mocking-the-faith memes (Nov 6 rule). Family-friendly reactions only.
- **Scripture beats get NO memes** — verse plaque + soft chime or silence only (Nov 6 rule).

## 3. The four placements

### 3.1 Corner / beside-the-head pop-up (DEFAULT — the Carter grammar)
Named for Jessica Carter's talking-head grammar, decoded in the Nov 6 pack (ref `DaEhPZuxsPl`): small reaction stickers/GIFs pop into the **empty space above or beside Q's head**, inside the platform safe zone, exactly on the punch beat, while he keeps talking.
- Size: small — a garnish, roughly 15–30% of frame width.
- Dwell: ~1–3s (Nov 6 norm); pure reaction pops can be as short as 0.5–1.5s (45-pairings norm).
- Entry/exit: pop or slide with a matched sound in AND out (see §5).
- Several per video is normal — each one earned by the exact line being spoken.

### 3.2 Poster slam (RATIONED — the Kilgore variant)
Named for Meg Kilgore's reel (ref `DdkJxYQvLUo`): ONE reaction GIF slams in **poster-size — top ~40–45% of the frame — ~1.5s, hard cut**, on the single biggest punch word of a section.
- Ration: ~2 per video maximum, biggest claims only. Its power is scarcity.

### 3.3 Full-frame transition meme (NEW Nov 12 — the YouTuber cutaway)
Q finishes a line; the video **hard-cuts to the meme full-frame** — the meme IS the transition into the next thought — then cuts back to Q. This is the Fireship mechanic, measured: inserts run **1–3s** and land **after each completed explanatory beat** — "memes are paragraph breaks, not jokes" (PandaStudio's frame-measured teardown: meme/stock inserts ≈35% of frames in that long-form style, always after a complete thought, never mid-clause).
- Use at thought boundaries: after the claim lands, after the story beat turns, before a new section.
- Never mid-sentence, never over speech that carries information — his next line starts when he's back on screen.
- SpongeBob time cards are this placement's story variant: full-screen transition jokes for actual time jumps ("started at 32… a few moments later") — **max 1 per video**, only where the story really jumps (Nov 6 rule). Banked: `library/time_cards/` (4 cards, 1.4–2.3s, silent — add their classic feel with a matched cue).

### 3.4 Reaction cutaway / emotion underline
Immediately after a line, a 0.8–2.5s reaction meme *answers* it: the shocked face after the number, the laughing clip after the funny fail, the slow-clap after the win, the side-eye after the bad advice he's debunking. It can ride as a pop-up (§3.1) or a brief full-frame cutaway (§3.3) — the grammar is the same: **the meme is the viewer's reaction, shown.**
- Emotion underline variant: the meme pops UNDER a claim (lower-third area, off the face) while he delivers the proof — reaction and evidence in the same beat.

## 4. Timing law

- **On the beat.** The meme lands ON the punch word or within ~2 frames after the triggering line ends — never early (spoils the line), never late (reads random). Meme entry is a beat-locked event like every other device in Q's system.
- **Dwell:** pop-ups 0.5–3s by type (§3); full-frame transitions 1–3s; poster slam ~1.5s. Gone before the next sentence needs the viewer's eyes.
- **Never covering information.** A meme never sits over speech that carries facts, numbers, steps, or the CTA. It punctuates completed thoughts; it does not compete with live ones.
- **Midpoint mode-switch:** retention data puts a deliberate pattern interrupt at the video's midpoint (second-biggest drop-off after the hook) — a meme transition is the cheapest strong interrupt for that spot.

## 5. Audio law

- **Every pop has a matched sound, in AND out** (Nov 6 rule — also Q's standing EVERYTHING-HAS-SOUND law). A silent meme pop is a failed event. Pairings live in `MEME_GRAMMAR_45_PAIRINGS.md` (cha-ching on money, vine boom on the single biggest claim, record scratch on the "wait—what" freeze, buzzer on the wrong way, ding on the right way, crickets on the nothing-happened beat, sad trombone on the light fail…).
- **Levels:** SFX sit **≈ −12 dB under the voice** for routine pops (Submagic/45-pairings norm), in a hierarchy — routine pops quiet, reveals/booms/buzzers louder, vine boom loudest and rarest. Meme **clip audio** (when the meme itself has a sound/line): if Q is talking over a pop-up, the meme's own audio is **muted or ducked well under his voice**; in a full-frame transition where he is NOT speaking, the meme's audio may play at full level — it IS the beat. If his voice continues under a cutaway, duck the meme audio so his words stay understood (MemeTap mixing rule: duck the bed when a spoken clip plays; effects slightly below the main voice, raised only for a deliberate punchline — StoryCut).
- **One sound family per job, consistent across videos** — same sound for the same job every time is what turns a pairing into a signature the audience learns.
- **Real recordings only** — banked meme sounds and the licensed library. Never synthesized SFX. (Banked so far: `library/sounds/` — applause, bruh, crickets, ticking clock; the Nov 6 refs name Bruh · Vine Boom · iPhone Marimba · Bad Gas · "Morality" sting as the iconic five to pull from cleared sources.)

## 6. Frequency discipline

- **Emotion peaks only.** A meme must answer the exact line being spoken ("why this, why now" — if it doesn't answer, cut it; a random meme is a defect, not coverage).
- Short-form working rhythm: a meme roughly **every 20–45s at most** in a 60–90s video (typically 2–4, plus at most ~2 poster slams and 1 time card) — Carter-style videos run denser ONLY when every pop is individually earned. Measured long-form (Fireship) runs an insert after nearly every beat, but that is faceless VO-only content; Q's face is the anchor, so his density stays lower.
- Never two memes within ~5s. Never a meme in the hook's first line unless it IS the hook device. Never under the CTA.
- 2026 correction (TheBeansMedia, in the 45-pairings doc): constant loud meme/SFX spam now reads as fatigue — subtle on routine beats, big only on the biggest moments.

**Cross-video rotation (Q, Nov 13, 2026 — the no-same-ol' rule).** Frequency discipline applies ACROSS videos, not just inside one: the same clip never runs in back-to-back videos or twice in a batch, and the same sound pairing does not become the default answer to the same beat type. Before placing, check the catalog's rotation ledger/cooldown list and take the least-recently-used fitting clip. When the only fitting options are on cooldown, source + screen a new clean clip for that beat — or place no meme. Repetition is the defect; absence is not.

## 7. Sizing / position law

- **NOTHING over Q's face. Ever.** Memes live in empty frame space; if there is no clean space at that beat, skip the meme (Nov 6 rule — and Q's standing nothing-over-face law).
- Pop-ups: above/beside the head, upper corners, or lower-third underline — inside the IG/TikTok safe zone, clear of captions and of the caption band.
- Full-frame only as transitions/cutaways (§3.3/§3.4), never as a held overlay while he teaches.
- Memes keep their own look — **do not force PWC teal/gold/navy onto a meme.** The joke is recognition; recoloring kills it. Brand dressing belongs to Q's own cards and graphics around the meme, not on it.

## 8. What memes never do

- Never substitute for proof — receipts, payout screens, Skool screens stay real and sharp per the blur law; memes season AROUND proof.
- Never imply endorsement — a meme of a known person is a reaction, not that person endorsing Q or PWC (attribution honesty law).
- Never on scripture beats. Never profane (audio or text). Never mean.
- Never wallpaper — if removing the meme changes nothing, it shouldn't have been there.
