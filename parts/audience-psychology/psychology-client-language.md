## The rule
Never teach in coach jargon — write the client's day-to-day situation in
THEIR words. If the audience would glaze over, rewrite it in their sentence.
The test is recognition: the viewer must feel "you get me better than
anybody else."

## The devices
1. **Client-language example** — not "nervous system regulation, somatic
   work, trust yourself" (abstract language they glaze over) but "every
   time the phone goes, they immediately think it's going to be bad news."
   The client's sentence IS the content. Q's scripts already demand the
   avatar's real words; this is the sharpest test of it.
2. **Micro-moments over mansion things** — not the big abstract outcome but
   "waking up and not worrying about your family all weekend", "anxiety
   isn't in their belly anymore" — small specific outcomes the audience
   "would kill for." Q's proof content should name the small moment, not
   just the big number.
3. **The two focuses** — (1) your own story: model your own before →
   micro-moments of the after; (2) why your way is different: the 4 methods
   they tried → why they haven't worked → why yours is the final solution.
   Maps onto Q's proof-led + authority content: story first,
   differentiation second.
4. **The 4-methods test** — "what are four different methods your ideal
   client has tried to heal their anxiety with? Why haven't they worked?
   And why does your way become the final solution for them?" A
   differentiation device Q can run for any niche.
5. **The ladder** — "taking your audience from 'that sounds good' to 'Oh
   my god, I can't live without this.'" Every post should move the reader
   one rung — from nodding to needing it.
