## The rule
The avatar is not a demographic. It is a feeling state: posting for free,
guessing the rate, pitching into silence, watching bigger creators get
paid for the same work. Every video must name that feeling in the
viewer's own words in the first line — "posting for free," "filming in
the fifteen minutes before a shift" — before it teaches anything. A
viewer who feels seen stays; a viewer who feels lectured scrolls. The
avatar does not buy a course. They buy the end of a specific humiliation:
working for free.

## What they fear (say it in their words)
1. **"I'm posting for free and nobody's paying me."** The core wound.
   A 30-second video is several hours of work; the pay is not. (Pain #8)
2. **"What do I charge?"** Rate negotiation is the #1 challenge — 50%
   of creators call it their biggest challenge. Guessing feels like
   exposure: charge too much and lose the deal, too little and be
   exploited. (Pain #1)
3. **"They'll ghost me / pay me late / never pay me."** 62% rank timely
   pay #1; 1/3–9/10 paid late; net-90 reads as a free loan. (Pain #2)
4. **"They'll offer me product instead of money."** Gifted-only is
   banned in r/UGCcreators; $150/video is the floor the community
   itself enforces. (Pain #3)
5. **"I'll do endless revisions for one fee."** "One small edit" = a
   reshoot; 31% call it a top pain. (Pain #4)
6. **"Everyone says something different."** Conflicting advice — "one
   person says to do one thing, the other says the opposite" (month 4,
   worked hard, "I'm PISSED"). (Pain #7)
7. **"Every month starts at zero."** No retainer, no pipeline, no
   system — each month is a new scramble. (Pain-points month-zero part)
8. **"I'm too small / too late."** Under 10K feels invisible. The
   counter-evidence is the market itself: nano creators have the
   HIGHEST engagement of any tier (10.3% on TikTok) and 73% of brands
   now prefer them — the avatar's smallness is the asset, and they do
   not know it yet.

## What they want (in order)
1. **One paid deal.** Not a career — one. The first $150–$400 video
   proves the whole thing is real. Every proof video should make the
   first deal feel one step away, not one year away.
2. **A rate they can say out loud.** A number with math behind it, not
   a guess. "Your rate is a system, not a guess" is the promise.
3. **A pitch that gets answered.** The portfolio kills the pitch: 3 top
   videos, not 300. They want the 3.
4. **A system, not more tips.** They have tips. They do not have a
   loop: post → pitch → get paid → repeat. The 90-day path (BUILD →
   CREATE → MONETIZE → SCALE) is the system, and it must be visible.
5. **People like them getting paid.** Plural proof — the wall of other
   small creators' paid videos — beats any single receipt, because it
   answers "will this work for someone like ME?"

## What they buy (and what stops them)
- They buy **the end of posting for free** — framed as the system, the
  room, the cohort ("the 90-day path from posting for free to
  deal-ready"), never "a course I'm teaching" (solution positioning).
- They buy **after free teaching proves it** — the waitlist nurture
  (DM broadcasts + avatar warm-ups) exists so they can judge before
  paying a dollar. "Doors are closed until Dec 7 — you're not buying
  anything today" is the strongest close line in the library, because
  it removes the risk of buying.
- What stops them, in their words: "I can't afford $97/mo" (answer:
  monthly only, no annual, extra month free for waitlisters); "Is this
  another course?" (answer: a cohort with two lives a week and hot
  seats — the community is the product); "I've bought things before
  that didn't work" (answer: judge the free teaching first); "I have
  to talk to my spouse about it" (answer: built by a couple, around
  kids and a household budget — decide together before paying a
  dollar). Full table: `research/knowledge/SALES-CLOSING.md`.

## Executable steps
1. Every script's first line names the avatar in THEIR words ("creators
   under 10K, posting for free") — never "what this video is about."
   Shannon's test: would the viewer think "okay, he gets me"?
2. Every video answers one fear with one proof: fear → proof object on
   screen at the claim second. Fear without proof is venting; proof
   without fear is bragging.
3. Never invent a fear. No public source + verbatim quote = not in the
   file. The weekly re-sweep (r/UGCcreators, r/VideoEditing, IG
   captions, complaint threads) is the source of new fears — log
   verbatim + URL + date.
4. The avatar's smallness is the selling point: cite the market (nano
   10.3% engagement, 73% of brands prefer micro) whenever the script
   touches "I'm too small." The numbers are public and current (2026).
5. The close is always one small specific action (comment one keyword /
   drop an email), never a big decision. Big decisions happen in the
   nurture, not in the reel.

## Worked example (PWC)
RC1 "Three Deals at 500 Followers": fear named in line 1 — "500
followers and brands still won't pay you?" Proof: @nathanmthomas, 500
followers, 3 brand deals, $1,900 in one week (real creator, @handle
visible, never blurred). Want answered: the first deal is one system
away. Close: Comment WAITLIST. The avatar sees someone SMALLER than
them getting paid — the fear "I'm too small" dies on screen.
