# PSYCHOLOGY OF CONTENT — Why people watch, stop, share, save, comment (subject a)

Source: retentionyt.com "How to Script a YouTube Video That Keeps Viewers
Watching" (Loewenstein information-gap theory, Zeigarnik effect; average
YouTube video retains 23.7% by the final frame, 55% gone before 1 minute) ·
tukey.ai "YouTube Hook Formula" (3.3B Shorts analysed: 50–60% of all drop-off
in the first 3 seconds; 71% decide keep-or-leave in the first 3s; Stanford
pattern-interrupt 0.3s attention grab; dopamine rises with reward-timing
uncertainty) · overseeros.com "The YouTube Hook Framework" (7 hook types;
steepest drop-off in the first 15–30s) · toptal.com "How to Make Good
Instagram Reels" (shares + saves weighted above likes/comments; >50% of
Reel views from non-followers) · lilachbullock.com (watch time is the #1
signal per Adam Mosseri; shares are the most undervalued metric — a DM
share puts the video in front of a non-follower with an implicit
recommendation attached) · @techwith_komal IG reel DanTWPtJVgu (save ≈ 10×
a like in ranking value; 80% completion beats 10,000 likes). Builds on
`parts/hooks/` (Adilet patterns, Ava 7 types, hookscore 26 formulas) and
`parts/audience-psychology/audience-psychology-four-levers.md` — this part
is the WHY behind those hook patterns, not a second hook list.

## The rule
Every action a viewer takes is a different psychological job. Watch =
curiosity + relevance. Stop = the loop closed too early or never opened.
Share = identity ("this is me / this is for you"). Save = utility ("I need
this later"). Comment = belonging or a reward ("say the word, get the
thing"). A video that optimises only for watch time gets watched and
forgotten; a video built for all five jobs gets distributed.

## The five jobs (with the numbers behind them)
1. **Watch — the information gap.** Loewenstein (1994): curiosity is the
   gap between what you know and what you want to know. The hook must
   expose a gap the viewer already cares about — not a vague mystery.
   55% of viewers are gone before 1 minute; 50–60% of Shorts drop-off
   happens in the first 3 seconds. The first 3 seconds decide.
2. **Stop — the loop closed or never opened.** Viewers leave when they
   can leave without missing anything. If the payoff lands at 0:08 and
   nothing new is promised, the video is over at 0:08 no matter how long
   it runs. Every closed loop must open the next one before it closes
   (see `psychology-open-loops-payoff-timing.md`).
3. **Share — identity + social currency.** People share what says
   something about them or helps someone specific. A DM share is a
   personal endorsement to a non-follower — the highest-value
   distribution on Instagram. Share-bait is a line a viewer can send to
   ONE person: "this is you," "we need this," "remember when."
4. **Save — utility + incompleteness.** Saves are worth ~10× a like in
   ranking. People save what they will need again: a rate sheet, a pitch
   template, a checklist, a number they want to quote. A video with
   nothing saveable is a video with nothing to return to. Put the
   saveable thing ON SCREEN (a board, a list, a figure) — audio alone
   does not get saved.
5. **Comment — belonging + reciprocity.** A comment is a public act of
   joining. The keyword mechanic (Comment WAITLIST → DM) works because
   it is low-effort reciprocity: one word gets a free resource, and the
   comment itself tells the algorithm people are engaging (Bradford
   Marais, DfFUHjDqewW decode). Comments > likes is also a funnel
   signal: a post with more comments than likes is a conversion post,
   not a reach post.

## Executable steps
1. Before writing any script, name the ONE job this video must do best
   (watch / share / save / comment) and the second job it must also do.
   Write both in the script header. A video with no named job is a
   video optimised for nothing.
2. Watch-job check: does the first 3 seconds expose a specific gap
   ("the one change that took my retention from 28% to 61% has nothing
   to do with editing" — tukey.ai example shape)? If the hook could
   belong to any video, rewrite it.
3. Share-job check: is there one line a viewer would send to exactly
   one friend? If not, add a sendable line — a claim about THEM, not
   about Q.
4. Save-job check: is there one on-screen object worth returning to
   (rate table, 3-step list, proof figure)? If the video is audio-only
   value, it will not be saved — put the value on a board.
5. Comment-job check: does the close ask for ONE word that delivers a
   named thing ("Comment MATH and I'll send the rate sheet")? The
   keyword flow must exist BEFORE the post (standing rule).
6. After posting, read the ratio, not the count: comments > likes =
   funnel post working; saves > comments = utility post working;
   shares > saves = identity post working. Feed the winning job back
   into the next script in the same lane.

## Worked example (PWC)
P3 "One Month 28800": watch job = "$28,800 in one month" (gap: how?);
share job = "creators like my wife and I earn like this — why can't
you?" (identity: the viewer sends it to a creator friend); save job =
the $200/$400 flagged-payment rows on screen (the small payments that
add up — worth returning to); comment job = Comment WAITLIST → 3-Wins
PDF. Four jobs in one 30-second video; the proof is the shareable,
saveable, commentable object.

## PWC skin
- Second-person framing binds: the gap, the share line, and the saveable
  board are all about YOU (the viewer), never about Q's own earnings
  stated directly. Collective framing ("creators like my wife and I…")
  is the allowed shape for earnings claims.
- Teal #14A886 + gold #FEE238 on navy #00214E, NO RED. Nothing over
  the face. CapCut-native motion.
- Never manufacture a gap with a false claim. The hook's gap must close
  on a TRUE payoff in the same video (hookscore kill-gate still binds).

## GAPS
- The 23.7% / 55% / 3.3B-Shorts figures are YouTube-wide benchmarks
  from the cited guides, not Q's own analytics — use them as direction,

  Instagram's ranking, not a Meta-published weight — direction, not
