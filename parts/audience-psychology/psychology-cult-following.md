## The rule
People do not stay for tips. They stay because leaving would mean
leaving WHO THEY ARE. A cult-like following is built from four
ingredients in a fixed order: (1) a transformation bigger than the
product, (2) a shared story with a shared enemy, (3) markers of
belonging members can see and use, (4) rituals that make membership
real every week. Skip the transformation and you have a fan club. Skip
the enemy and you have a classroom. Skip the markers and you have an
audience. Skip the rituals and you have a launch, not a community.

## The four ingredients (PWC mapping)
### 1. Language — the tribe's words
- Keywords are badges: WAITLIST, READY, MATH, QUIET, GUIDE, CALENDAR,
  SKILL, CAPTIONS, EFFECTS. Typing WAITLIST in public is saying "I'm
  one of us." Same words in videos, captions, DMs, and the Skool
  classroom — the language is continuous everywhere Q shows up.
- Coined terms only insiders understand: "posting for free" (the
  trap), "deal-ready in 90 days" (the destination), "the wall" (the
  proof). Forced jargon feels manipulative — every term must name
  something real the avatar already feels.
- Rule: keep the keyword list short enough to remember. The 47 live
  flows are the ceiling, not the goal.

### 2. Symbols — markers of belonging members can see
- The PWC badge (teal/gold on navy) on every video, every cover, every
  end card. The Premium dots (🟡🔴🟠) signal depth without saying
  "premium only." The 90-day path (BUILD → CREATE → MONETIZE →
  SCALE) is a visible map with a visible end (Day 90 / Graduation).
- The wall itself is a symbol: 15 real creator tiles that say "people
  like you are already getting paid." Proof is always plural and
  always real — one receipt is a claim; a wall is a community.
- Rule: every member must be able to say where they are on the path.
  If the path is invisible, the community is just a chat room.

### 3. Rituals — the same thing, same time, every time
- Two lives a week: Mon 12 PM CT Premium teaching Webinar + Fri 12 PM
  CT Premium Lab/Hot Seats. One Friday a month the Lab opens to
  Everyone. The daily three posts, the 7 AM E-series slot, the 9 PM
  backyard lane (closed Nov 8 — the lane is gone, the ritual lesson
  stands).
- A ritual must survive a bad week. If it only happens when energy is
  high, it's not a ritual, it's a mood. Record-ahead drops on
  low-energy weeks protect the ritual without faking the energy.
- Rule: 1–2 signature series at a time, consistent like a weekly
  episodic TV show. A new series starts only when an old one ends or
  Q rules it in — never stack five rituals and keep none.
- The 3-story daily (Shannon, NEEDS-Q): greet → promote → personal.
  Stories are where conversion happens (Ron Smith — see
  `psychology-buyers-community.md`).

### 4. Myths — the shared story, retold
- Q's origin (Baltimore → Houston on faith, 4 kids + dog + cat, no
  village, started at 32; Kiya wanted to be a creator since 14) is not
  a bio — it is the avatar's story told in advance. The audience sees
  their own move in his move. Retold in LAUNCH/BIO/ORIGIN, referenced
  everywhere else, never performed as a flex.
- The common enemy is the SYSTEM (posting for free, working for
  product, guessing your rate) — never a named creator or brand, never
  a person. A stance against a BAD IDEA is fine; a stance against a
  PERSON is not (polarizing-stance caution — NEEDS-Q before any
  polarizing hook is used).
- Turning points + inside jokes + signature language = shared
  mythology (Jayda Jones). The mythology must be TRUE: every story
  Q tells is a real event, every number a real receipt.

## Why people stay — and why they recruit others
- **Identity shift (Isa Vrolijk):** followers internalize the brand;
  leaving feels like a personal loss. The shift is "I am a creator who
  gets paid" — not "I watch a coach." Every video must move the
  viewer one inch toward that sentence.
- **Recognition + status:** member-generated education — members
  teaching members — gives status and reasons to contribute. Hot
  seats, member wins posted as proof, leaderboard progression on
  Skool. The wins members post become the proof that fills the next
  month's content (the loop that compounds).
- **Mutual support:** the community creates value for itself. A member
  who answers another member's pitch question is doing retention work
  no broadcast can do.
- **Initiation:** joining must feel earned, not bought. The waitlist →
  Dec 7 join link in DM → Day 1 live is an initiation sequence: value
  first, email, nurture, door opens, first in line, extra month free.
  Early access and a memorable onboarding (the 3-Wins PDF + the first
  7 days mapped) make participation feel meaningful.
- **Recruitment is identity, not referral links:** people recruit when
  belonging is visible — when they can show a friend the wall, the
  language, the ritual, and say "this is us." Make the belonging
  showable: the wall, the badge, the keyword.

## Executable steps
1. Every video passes the belonging check (Specialist #13): does it
   name the avatar in line 1? Does it use the tribe's words? Does it
   end open, pointing at the next thing? Is the proof plural and real?
   A fail on any one = re-edit, not ship.
2. Every month, audit the four ingredients: is the language still
   short and real? Are the symbols visible? Did every ritual happen
   on time? Was the story retold (not just assumed)? A missing
   ingredient gets a fix that month, not a new mechanic.
3. Never invent a ritual, a term, or a story. New mechanics are flagged
   NEEDS-Q, never auto-adopted. Forced jargon and arbitrary rituals
   feel manipulative — and the audience can tell.
4. Study one creator with a cult-like following per week (Specialist
   #13's standing job): the creator, the mechanic, the evidence
   (a retention signal, not just a follower count), and whether it fits
   PWC. File one paragraph each; fold what fits, park what doesn't
   with the reason.
