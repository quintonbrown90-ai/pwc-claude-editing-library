## The rule
An open loop is a debt the video owes the viewer. Open it early, carry
it visibly, pay it exactly when promised — and open the next one before
the last one closes. A video with one loop is a video with one reason to
stay; a video with layered loops (a big loop for the whole video, small
loops per section) is a video the viewer cannot leave mid-way without
feeling the itch. A loop opened and never closed is not a cliffhanger —
it is a broken promise, and the viewer remembers it next time they see
Q's face.

## The loop vocabulary (use these labels in every beat map)
| Loop label | What it is | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| Promise | The end result shown before the how ("you see the finished doc at 0:07") | Hook, 0:00–0:03 |
| "I can do this" | Real tool names / concrete steps make it feel doable; pace slows so you can follow (gets saves + rewatches) | Setup section |
| Self-relevance | The viewer pictures THEIR niche / THEIR page in the frame | Run/demo section |
| Reveal + modeled reaction | Show what happens behind the scenes + tell the viewer how to feel about it ("It's wild") | Mechanism section |
| Proof | Deliver what the hook promised; the loop from 0:03 closes | Payoff, ~75–85% through |
| Extra value | One more upgrade just when you think it's over — rewards people who watched to the end | After the payoff |
| Reciprocity, low effort | One word gets a free resource; the ask costs almost nothing | CTA, final seconds |

## Executable steps
1. **One big loop per video, named in the script header.** Format:
   "LOOP: [question the video owes an answer to] — CLOSES AT: [timestamp
   / beat]." If the loop cannot be written as one question, the video
   has no loop — re-brief it.
2. **Kallaway check on the hook:** Context (immediate, audio + visuals
   + text aligned) → Lean (curiosity in a direction — the viewer leans
   in) → Contrarian Snapback (jerk the viewer down another path).
   Read the first 1–2 sentences back on their own: is the context
   clear, and is there something curiosity-inducing? If not, rewrite.
3. **Layer small loops per section.** Each section opens its own
   question and closes it — but the close of a small loop must open (or
   point at) the next one. Never close two loops in the same beat with
   nothing open after them; that is where the viewer leaves.
4. **Payoff timing:** the big loop closes at 75–85% of the video, never
   in the first 15% and never after the last 10%. A payoff at 0:08 in a
   0:40 video leaves 32 seconds of dead obligation. A payoff that
   never lands is a lie. After the payoff: one Extra-value beat, then
   the CTA — in that order.
5. **Dopamine discipline:** imply the reward, do not explain it. "I'll
   show you the exact template at the end" holds attention; "the
   template has five steps: step one is…" in the hook spends the
   reward before the viewer has earned it. Withhold the mechanism,
   never the outcome — the viewer must know WHAT they will get, not
   HOW it works, until the payoff.
6. **Loop audit on the exported file (QC):** list every loop the video
   opened and the timestamp each closed. Any loop with no close = FAIL,
   re-edit. Any close with no next loop opened before it (except the
   final CTA) = a drop-off point — note it in the QC report.

## Worked example (PWC)
P1 "Brands Are Paying Small Creators Like You": big loop — "how do
brands pay creators with 500 followers?" (opens 0:00, closes at the
wall of 15 real creator tiles + the $1,900 figure). Small loops: "which
creator?" (closes on each tile) → "how much?" (closes on the figure) →
"how do I start?" (closes on Comment WAITLIST). Promise before how:
the wall is visible in the first 2 seconds. Extra value after payoff:
the rate math. CTA: one word, WAITLIST.
