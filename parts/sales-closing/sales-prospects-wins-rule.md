## The rule
"Your wins don't matter to them. Their wins do." Q's second-person framing
law ("you could be making this") in one line — the sharpest statement of it
found in the harvest. Credentials are a quick highlight, then the whole pitch
pivots to "about you."

## The format: roleplay-then-deconstruct
Demo the pitch live, then break down why it worked — the demo IS the
lesson. Q's objection/closing teaching can use the same shape (real DM/call
first, breakdown second).
1. **The roleplay** — "If I was going to talk to you about my sales
   consulting, I might give you a quick highlight of... my experience, but
   more so I want this to be about you."
2. **The credentials flash** — one line of proof ("I am a closer for a
   Fortune 50 company, I have won multiple President's Circle awards"),
   then the pivot. Proof, then "about you."
3. **The pivot line** — "but more so I want this to be about you." The
   pivot from his resume to their outcome is a physical opening (open
   palms) — the call is theirs.
4. **Their win, painted concretely** — "can I take you from where you're at
   right now to making 10K months" / sales awards / trips to Palm Beach,
   Santa Barbara. The win is theirs, the proof is yours.
5. **The end-goal question** — "so here's my question for you, what is
   your end goal?" One question that makes the prospect state the outcome,
   which the pitch then serves. The DM/call closer.
6. **The deconstruction** — what NOT to do: "I'm the greatest, you should
   follow me" + objection-overcoming as a performance — "no, that's
   garbage." (Hand chop on the verdict.)
