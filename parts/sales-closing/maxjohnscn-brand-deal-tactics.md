## The rule
A brand reply is a quote, not a number. Quote high, raise on the yes,
and cap revisions at one round — the reply does the negotiating before
the negotiation starts.

## The three tactics (verbatim in substance)
1. **Reply with a full quote, not a one-liner.** He used to reply with
   one line: just his rate. Now every reply includes: his rate + the cost
   of cross-posting + the cost to run it as an ad + when he wants to be
   paid by. His claim: deals that "used to drag on for a month could now
   get done in under a week." Q's teaching point: rate + cross-post cost
   + ad-usage cost + payment terms in EVERY brand reply (Q's own
   rates/terms).
2. **Quote way more than you'd accept — and raise when brands say yes
   first time.** He was asking ~$700/video and usually accepting ~$500.
   Rule: every time brands say yes to the first offer, that is the signal
   to raise the rate. One-line rate-setting rule for the PWC rate-setting
   module (Q's numbers).
3. **One round of revisions only — for the script AND the video.** Before:
   brands made him change the video over and over, "back and forth for
   weeks." Now: one change to the script, one change to the video, max.
   A contract term for the PWC pitch/rate pack.
