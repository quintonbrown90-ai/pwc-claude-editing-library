## The rule
"Sales is a transfer of certainty, belief. When I'm willing to give you my
money, I'm telling you I believe in you that you can get me where I want to
be." Certainty is the product, not the pitch. Directly usable in Q's
closing/DM scripts.

## The perfect-question trap
Reps hunt for the perfect question and miss the context the prospect is
giving them — "they're not listening. They're thinking the whole time." The
prospect then "water-hoses" you with information and you don't know what to
do with it "because you're not really paying attention as to why you're
asking the question." Why it happens: reps copy questions from sales
trainers they admire, "but those questions come from that person rather
than from you" — they lack "the necessary internal characteristics and
resiliency" and the confidence/certainty of the person they copied.

## The fixes
1. **The 3-part opener** — "Tell me what you do, how it works, and who you
   help." Three lines, no interrogation. A DM/call opener template.
2. **The tool belt of questions** — questions built from the client journey,
   each with a known WHY, that "move the conversation forward in a
   productive way where it doesn't feel like an interrogation." Script-QC
   rule for Q's setter/DM scripts: "if you don't understand why you're
   asking the question, you should not be asking the question." The cause
   is lack of product knowledge: "They don't truly understand what they do
   and how it works."
3. **The fix path** — "Get into your school, get into the community...
   fully understanding the journey of the client. The more you understand
   that journey, the more you can build a tool belt of questions."
4. **The close** — "if you can't confidently say that you know how to
   deliver the result for the client and that your product solves their
   problem, you shouldn't be selling it. Use that as a challenge."
