## The rule
Hook claim → "Watch this" → worked example, on screen, step by step, as the
video → name the phenomenon AFTER the proof, never before. The example IS
the teaching.

## The template (both reels, same skeleton)
1. **Hook claim (≤3s)** — "Did you know that if you take any four digit
   number... your number will always end up flattening to 6174." / "Did you
   know that there's a number that when you multiply it by 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6,
   it just gives you the exact same digits back, just rotated?"
2. **"Watch this" handoff** — the worked example starts. The example is
   played out on screen, step by step, with the numbers as characters.
3. **Named-trap beat** — "Don't drop that leading zero" — the one mistake
   viewers will make, called out mid-example, before they make it. The trap
   is the retention.
4. **Break-the-pattern beat** — run the pattern until it breaks, then the
   break IS the reveal: "But what if you multiply it by 7? You get
   999,999... that's the giveaway for where this number comes from." The
   exception is the origin story.
5. **Try-it-yourself checks** — split-and-add party tricks (142+857=999,
   14+28+57=99): two quick checks the viewer can do in their head before
   the why lands. The check is the participation.
6. **Name it AFTER the proof** — "It's called Kaprekar's Constant" /
   "That's called a cyclic number" — the name comes after the example, never
   before.
7. **Edge cases + scale proof** — 5,555 fails (all digits match); "they ran
   all 8,991 numbers that qualify through a computer, and not one took more
   than seven steps" (his claim, not verified).
8. **The why, then the metaphor close** — the funnel visual ("the whole
   thing works like a funnel for numbers") / the remainder wheel; then the
   technical term defined in one line ("a fixed point — a number that maps
   back onto itself") + a physical metaphor ("pulls everything like a
   magnet"). The metaphor is the memory.

## The device: pixel-art digit-characters
The digits are characters (holding tiles in a colosseum / on a classroom
rug) and the rotation IS the proof — the viewer sees the same six digits
slide over, no algebra needed. The abstract made into moving characters.
