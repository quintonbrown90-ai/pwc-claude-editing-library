# SCRIPTWRITING — Contrast device: show the slop first (Bradford Marais, INFERRED reconstruction)

Source: @bradfordmarais — canonical reel DdWcjF7Ky__ (f_013) + extended cut Ddq9JE2q4-B (f_010), Batch 5. The device is fully visible on screen; the format below is reconstructed from the frames + transcript, labeled INFERRED where it goes beyond what is shown.

## The rule
Show the generic AI output as the problem, THEN the system as the fix. Never describe the slop — show it, full-screen, in the AI's own words, before naming anything else. The viewer must recognise their own output in the slop before they care about the system.

## The anatomy (INFERRED from the two reels)
1. **Hook** — the promise ("Turn Claude into a master scriptwriter") + the this-vs-that frame ("scripts that give you this instead of this").
2. **The slop, full-screen** — the generic output shown as a document/card, not described. It runs long on purpose: the viewer reads enough to feel the genericness (greeting + "really exciting" + "unlock the full potential" + "in this fast-paced digital landscape" + "the possibilities are truly endless" — the tell-tale phrases).
3. **The diagnosis line** — one sentence naming WHY it is slop ("you're using Claude in the wrong step" / "without the three files"). The diagnosis lands AFTER the slop, never before.
4. **The system** — the layers/steps, each with its own proof device (Brand Brain card / Playbook card / Quality card; or the 4 steps).
5. **The close** — one-word comment keyword.

## Executable steps (Q's teaching content)
1. Pick the generic output to show: it must be REAL generic output (generated cold, no Brand Brain, no Playbook) — never a strawman you wrote to look bad. Generate it live, show the prompt that made it.
2. Show it full-screen as a document/card for 2–4s — long enough to read the tell-tale phrases, short enough to stay in the reel's pace.
3. Deliver the diagnosis in one sentence, spoken, while the slop is still on screen or just after it leaves.
4. Show the system that fixes it — each layer with its own proof device. The fixed output (same prompt, with Brain + Playbook) can close the loop as a before/after.
5. Close with the keyword CTA.

## Worked example (PWC)
Q teaches "why your scripts sound like AI". Slop card (real cold output): "Hey guys! In today's video I'm going to share 5 amazing tips to grow on Instagram that will literally change your life…" Diagnosis, spoken: "That's not a script, that's a greeting with tips attached — Claude had no idea who you are, what you sell, or how you talk." System: Brand Brain (3 files) → Playbook (proof-led structure) → Quality Gate. Fixed output shown beside the slop: same topic, Q's voice, proof in first 2s, Comment WAITLIST. The contrast IS the lesson.
