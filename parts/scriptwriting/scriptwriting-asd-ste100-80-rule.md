## The rule
Follow ASD-STE100 about 80% of the way — most of the clarity, none of the
robot tone. ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English) is the writing standard
the aerospace industry built so maintenance manuals could never be misread.
"All of the models already know it and understand it fully" is HIS claim,
not verified — record as observed.

## The 80% rule (the writing standard, applied at 80%, never 100%)
- Max 20 words a sentence.
- Approved words only.
- Active voice.
- One instruction per sentence.
At 100% it "kind of starts sounding like a robot" — so apply it 80% and
keep the natural tone. Q's Scriptwriting lane applies the same way: the 80%
is the rule.

## The output ladder (ask for the format, not the paragraph)
From Andrej Karpathy (per Kayvon — on Anthropic's pre-training team; his
claim, recorded as observed):
1. **Write** — ask the LLM to explain something in ASD-STE100.
2. **Diagram** — "clean writing still has to be read, so instead you ask
   for a diagram and it's way easier to take in."
3. **Web page** — "just ask for the output in HTML... you ask for a
   calculator with sliders and you get a working page back instead of just
   a paragraph."
4. **Explainer video** — "fully custom explainer video on whatever topic
   that you want... create a 3Blue1Brown style video explainer on this and
   use my ElevenLabs API key for the narration." The one he's most bullish
   on.

## "Who does the work?" — the 90/10 graphic
"The model 90% / You 10%" — the models do more and more of the leg work;
the human job moves to making sure it's right. Q's verify-don't-write
framing: the 10% is the judgment.

## "Big custom throwaway software"
"Intelligence and code are cheap now so you can ask for big custom throwaway
software that would never have made sense to build before." The throwaway is
the product — ask for the one-off tool.
