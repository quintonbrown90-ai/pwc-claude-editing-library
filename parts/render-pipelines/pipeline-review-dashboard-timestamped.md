# RENDER PIPELINES — the review dashboard: comments with timestamps

Source: @aasil_khan_ reel Deb-a9sz9Hw (PUBLISHED, spoken verbatim).
The dashboard was built in 2 minutes on screen ("in just two minutes
it made an exact replica of frame.io") — his timing claim, recorded as
his. No file, no prompt, and no repo are shown — the ask is the spec.
The "$300 client video" and the frame.io "$1.275 billion acquisition"
figures are his, not verified.

## The rule
"Can you give me a frame.io style dashboard where I can add comments
with timestamps." Play the video, move around, add comments, share
with the team — the review lives ON the timeline, not in a chat thread.

## Executable steps
1. **Ask for it in one line:** the whole spec is the ask — a
   frame.io-style dashboard where comments are added with timestamps.
   (Same one-line-ask shape as the one-prompt edit: the ask carries
   the spec, the build follows it.)
2. **What the dashboard does:** play the video; move around the
   timeline; add a comment at a timestamp; share it with the team.
   Every note is pinned to a frame, so "the caption clips at 0:12"
   is a pin at 0:12, not a sentence in a thread that has to be
   re-found.
3. **The review loop it enables:** watch → pin notes at timestamps →
   Claude (or the editor) fixes each pin in order → re-watch the pins
   only, not the whole video. A pin is closed when the frame at that
   timestamp passes, not when someone says it is fixed.
4. **Where it sits in Q's pipeline:** after the render, before the
   approval card. Muse's QC pins go on the dashboard (freeze at 36.7s,
   board cross-fade at the verdict entrance, dead air after the last
   word); Q's notes pin beside them; the fix list is the pins, in
   timestamp order. Nothing ships with an open pin.

## Worked example (PWC)
P1 v9 review: pin at 54–56s ("the 381-view tile vanishes when he says
'a tile like this'"), pin at the hook ("spoken hook missing — title
shows, voice doesn't say it"), pin at the end card ("card lands 2.2s
after the last word — pull it to ≤1s"). Three pins, three fixes, one
re-watch of the pins. Keyword WAITLIST.

## Human-made check
A timestamped pin is a checkable claim — anyone can open the frame and
see whether the fix landed. A chat-thread note is not. The dashboard
is the difference between "fixed it" and "here, at 0:12, it is fixed."

## PWC skin
Dashboard in PWC palette (navy ground, teal pins, gold closed-pins);
no red for open pins — open pins are gold-outlined, closed pins are
teal-filled. Nothing in the dashboard names the hidden app; review
notes use Q's words, not the creator's.
