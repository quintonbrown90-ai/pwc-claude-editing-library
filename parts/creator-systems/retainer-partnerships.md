## The rule
Recurring monthly brand work beats one-off deals. Not a million one-off
brand deals — a select handful of brands on retainer, month after month.
Directly relevant to Q's PWC monetize lane and the agency context (Mase
pitching $10K+ deals).

## The model
A retainer client "just comes back to the same services month after month
after month" — recurring revenue, not deal-chasing. Creators can have
retainer clients too, especially educational creators "repeatedly talking
about a handful of select tools" — the niche + the repeated tools ARE the
retainer pitch.

## The devices (stealable, in order)
1. **Assumption-break hook** — state the money claim, name what the viewer
   assumes it means, break the assumption: "you might assume that means
   I'm taking brand partnerships from like a million different companies.
   But what if I told you I only work with this select handful of brands?"
   Q's proof-led posts already open with proof; the steal is the explicit
   assumption-break as the second beat.
2. **"The key, the secret, the hack" triple** — three synonyms stacked, then
   the one-word answer (retainer partnerships). A punchier reveal than a
   single "the secret is."
3. **Delayed self-intro** — hook first, name at 17s: "In case you haven't
   met yet, hi, my name's Kate." The hook earns the intro, not the other
   way round. Q's talking heads can do the same.
4. **Steal-framing hook** — "STEAL MY six figure": permission to copy as
   the hook device. Same family as @willgirone Dfd2Uj_x2Bg ("I'm giving you
   permission to steal this strategy") in the same chunk: permission-to-copy

5. **"We don't know how to gatekeep"** — free value as identity, not just
   tactic. Q's free-value-first positioning is the same move; the steal is
