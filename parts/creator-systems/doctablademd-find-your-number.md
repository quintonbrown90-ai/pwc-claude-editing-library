## The rule
"Your $200 AI plan is a steal. For now." "We paid $1,000. It cost
$62,000." The gap between what you pay and what it would cost is the
proof — and the gap is shrinking, so build while it's cheap.

## Executable steps
1. **Find your number (slide 6/7 — the copy-and-paste prompt):**
   "Look at my local AI coding logs for the last 30 days (Claude Code:
   ~/.claude/projects, Codex: ~/.codex/sessions). Add up every token
   by model, including cache reads and writes. Price it at current API
   rates and list the per-million price you used for each model. My
   plan costs $___ a month. Show a table by model, the total, and how
   many times my plan [pays for itself]." LOCAL LOGS ONLY — the prompt
   reads files on the machine; it asks for no API key, no login, no
   credential. (The prompt text is quoted verbatim from the slide;
   the tail is cut off on screen — recorded as shown, not completed.)
2. **Show the gap as two bars (slide 2/7):** "WHAT WE PAY A MONTH —
   $1,000" (short bar) vs "SAME USAGE AT API PRICES — $61,997" (full
   bar). "Our last 30 days of AI coding, at API prices." "Same $200
   plans anyone can buy." "Heavy users get way more than they pay
   for." The gap is VISIBLE, not just stated — the short bar against
   the full bar is the device.
3. **Name the real gap (slide 4/7):** "Most people use AI like a
   chatbot." "CHATBOT: It answers. You still do the work." /
   "ORCHESTRATION: It builds. Systems you own that keep working." "We
   use the same $200 plans to build systems we own." "The advantage
   isn't access. It's orchestration." Everyone has the same plans; the
   gap is what you build with them.
4. **The window (slide 5/7):** "Build it while it's cheap." "Someone
   else is paying the difference." "Right now, investor money covers
   the gap. And it's already shrinking." The shrinking is the urgency.
   His example system: "MY CEO SCORECARD, BUILT WITH CLAUDE CODE" —
   "Start with Reached in 5 min: it's the first number below target"
   (leads 20 ▲ / reached-in-5-min 0% ▼ / connect rate 30% ▲ /
   bookings 4 ▲ / show rate 78% ▲) + "Bottleneck finder — finds my
   business's bottlenecks and hands me daily actions."
5. **Your turn (slide 7/7):** "So what's your number?" "YOUR USAGE AT
   API PRICES — ?" (empty bar — the viewer's number goes in the blank).
   "Run it, then come back and post your result." The close is a
   question with a blank bar, not a claim.

## Worked example (PWC)
Q runs the prompt on his own logs once a month: tokens by model,
priced at API rates, table + total + multiple of the plan cost. The
two bars (plan cost vs API-price value) go on the scorecard beside the
launch numbers. The point is operational, not content: know what the
builds actually cost while the plans are cheap. Keyword WAITLIST.

## Human-made check
The numbers come from local logs, not from a dashboard claim — anyone
can open ~/.claude/projects and re-add them. A gap that can't be
re-added from the logs is a claim, not a count.

## PWC skin
The scorecard uses PWC palette (teal ▲ on-target, gold ▼ below-target
— NO RED for below-target; the below-target number is gold, the fix
is the action, not an alarm). The gated usage prompt + 10 systems
stay gated (GAP). The Oct 30 pricing change is his claim, not a
deadline Q acts on.
