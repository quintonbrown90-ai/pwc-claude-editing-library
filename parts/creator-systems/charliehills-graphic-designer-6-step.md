## The rule
6 steps · 2 design skills · the same files for every graphic. The brand
is written down once, the examples teach the taste, and the winner goes
back into the examples so the next graphic starts closer.

## Executable steps
1. **Paste one prompt:** "Claude writes the file for you. You never
   paste it again." The prompt: "Add this to my CLAUDE.md, and create
   it if it doesn't exist" — then the four house rules below.
2. **Give Claude house rules (CLAUDE.md, four lines):** 1. "Before
   anything, read REFERENCE.md in full." 2. "No inventing. Every value
   comes from it." 3. "Ask, don't choose, if it isn't covered there."
   4. "Check yourself. Fix what fails, then show me." The ask-don't-
   choose line is the rule: the gap is the ask, never the choice.
3. **Write down your brand (REFERENCE.md):** "REFERENCE.md holds every
   value Claude is allowed to use." His fields: colours + what each one
   is for; fonts and spacing; name and photo (under the subtitle);
   Never: glow, strip, and the rest of his list.
4. **Add 5 graphics you like:** "Put them in one examples folder.
   Claude starts from the closest." Saved in: examples.
5. **Turn on the 2 design skills:** "Turn them on once. They load every
   time." infographic-lean ("Builds it in your brand") and
   remove-ai-design-slop ("Takes out 29 AI tells" — his count, not
   verified). PWC mapping: the PWC design system + designed-captions
   skills play these two roles; no third-party skill is installed.
6. **Ask for 3, keep 1:** "Never take the first version. Pick the one
   that looks most like you." Then **save the winner:** "It goes back
   into examples. The next graphic starts closer." Saved to: examples.
   Closer: "Claude checks its own work / Line 4 of your CLAUDE.md.
   Before you ever see it."

## Human-made check
Ask-for-3-keep-1 is the judgment step — the first version is never the
ship version, and the pick is "the one that looks most like you," not
the one that scores highest on a rubric.

## PWC skin
His REFERENCE.md colours (blue/black/grey) are replaced wholesale by
the PWC palette; his Never-list (glow, strip) is extended with Q's
standing rules (no red, nothing over the face, no slanted text, no
invented dollar figures). The design comparison slide (2025: 30 tools,
30 tabs open, one graphic; 2026: one chat, every graphic) is the same
system's before/after — variant, not a second part.
