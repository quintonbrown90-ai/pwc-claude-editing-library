## The rule
"When prompting, most of us just describe the task. What, in fact, we should be using [is] the deeper meaning behind the prompt." And: "The ceiling is your request. Not the model." A weak brief, not a weak model, is the limiter.

## The pattern (three moves)
1. **Task → goal.** "Instead of saying to Claude 'fix this bug', say to Claude 'keep this app bug free.'" Restate the task as the GOAL behind it before anything else. The goal is what the expert aims at; the task is one bug.
2. **Ask what a top expert would do.** "Just paste it in before any task, and it tells you what a top expert would say. Things that you would never have thought about." The expert is consulted BEFORE the work, not after it — "Like hiring a senior, not an intern."
3. **Review the prompt itself.** "It doesn't just give you advice. It actually reviews the whole prompt, so you're getting the expert version of it." The prompt is reviewed BEFORE it runs. The expert-version checklist (f_034, VERBATIM-FRAME): "Expert version — Raw traces / Profile it / Load test" — the expert version of "Why is checkout so slow?" is a checklist, not a paragraph.

## The iceberg (the diagram)
Below the surface of any task (f_009–f_011, VERBATIM-FRAME): "What a pro would do / The rawest material / Their methods & tools". The deeper meaning is what's UNDER the task. When a brief feels thin, run the iceberg: what would a pro do first, what is the rawest material, what methods and tools does the pro reach for. Those three lines ARE the deeper prompt.
