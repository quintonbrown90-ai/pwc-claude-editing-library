## The rule
Q's niche is not "content creation." It is the gap between two facts:
brands desperately need authentic short-form video from real people,
and the people who can make it do not know how to charge, pitch, or get
found. The niche wins on THREE edges: (1) smallness is the asset (nano
engagement beats mega 3×); (2) authenticity is the product (UGC that
doesn't look like an ad); (3) the system is the moat (a portfolio + a
rate + a pitch loop, not tips). Positioning is: "creators under 10K,
posting for free, going deal-ready in 90 days." Anything that does not
serve that sentence is off-niche.

## What wins in Q's niche right now (2026, measured)
1. **Micro over mega.** 73% of brands prefer micro/mid-tier; brands
   work with 33% more micro-influencers per year. The market moved
   TOWARD Q's avatar. Every video should say so — the avatar's
   smallness is not a handicap to overcome, it is the thing brands are
   buying.
2. **UGC over polished ads.** Brands pull budget from studio ads into
   UGC because it converts better and costs less. UGC ads see ~4×
   higher click-through than traditional display; 92% of consumers
   trust peer recommendations over brand messages. The winning UGC
   look is real, vertical 9:16, product handled in good light — not
   cinematic, not slop.
3. **Portfolio over follower count.** "3–5 sample clips in one or two
   niches beats 20 random clips." A beauty brand that sees only pet
   videos does not hire you. Q's teaching must always end on the
   portfolio: 3 clips, 1–2 niches, public, discoverable.
4. **Usage rights are the money.** 30-day paid usage +50–100% on base;
   whitelisting +50–100%; exclusivity +25–100%/mo. Beginners leave
   half the money on the table by quoting one flat fee. Every rate
   video must teach the add-ons, not just the base.
5. **The strategy gap is the opening.** 87% of brands use UGC but only
   16% have a dedicated UGC strategy. Brands are buying without a
   system — creators who arrive WITH a system (portfolio + rate +
   pitch) get hired first. That is the whole PWC pitch to brands, and
   the whole PWC pitch to creators.
6. **High-trust niches pay 2–3×.** Tech/SaaS ($300–$1,000+/video),
   fintech, health/fitness pay premium because expertise converts.
   Beauty/skincare is the highest-volume niche but the most competitive.
   Q's avatar should pick ONE niche and go deep — generalist UGC is a
   race to the bottom ($75 clips).

## Niche research (the weekly routine)
1. **Track the niche, not the feed.** Keep a tracked-creators list
   (Oleg's shape: creator usernames + follower count + reels/month +
   average views). Add the top creators in Q's niche (coaching,
   content strategists) and the top creators in the avatar's niches
   (the niches Q's members actually pitch).
2. **Top 3 in 30 days, per creator.** For each tracked creator, pull
   the top 3 videos of the past 30 days. Analyse: hook, retention
   mechanism, reward/final insight, script structure. Rank by
   outlier multiple (views ÷ creator's own median; >3× is a signal —
   strategy-outlier-multiple-swipe).
3. **Steal the mechanism, never the content.** Re-target the angle to
   Q's avatar ("the idea is the same, the angle is re-targeted" —
   Oleg). A hook that worked for a fitness creator becomes a hook for
   a UGC creator only if the psychology transfers.
4. **Pain-point mining is niche research.** The weekly sweep of
   r/UGCcreators, r/VideoEditing, IG captions, and complaint threads
   (creator-pain-points part) IS the niche scan: new pains = new
   topics = new hooks. Log verbatim + URL + date.
5. **Algorithm watch (Instagram especially).** Track what the platform
   rewards this month: saves/shares weight, watch-time signals,
   trial-reel behaviour, Stories conversion. When Instagram changes
   what it rewards, the edit changes with it — the weekly sweep
   carries the algorithm notes, not this part.

  to deal-ready in 90 days. Proof-led. Second-person. Collective
  framing for earnings.
