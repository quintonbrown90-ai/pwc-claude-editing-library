## The rule
"Remake this exact effect with one prompt in under 30 seconds." The
effect tracks your body on every frame so it stays glued to you at all
times — and the prompt is long because every guess is a chance for
things to go wrong.

## Executable steps
1. **The doctrine (long prompts beat short prompts):** "All the reels
   that you watch make it seem so easy to generate amazing edits with
   one simple prompt. But… every time you send Claude a couple lines
   in the chat, it has to guess what you want, and every guess is
   another chance for things to go wrong." The fix: "stop giving [it]
   short prompts" — a long, exact code prompt is what makes the effect
   premium. His A/B proof: the same effect twice — "First up, regular
   Claude. It's flat, lifeless and looks amateur. But now, Claude Code
   with our bulletproof prompt. Night and day difference."
2. **The three steps (his words):** Step one — "log in to GitHub and
   search for my repo called X-ray effect and download it. It's
   completely free and contains a code prompt with over 30,000
   characters of code, so it's going to be super accurate." Step two —
   "open Claude, click on the code button, and then drag in the GitHub
   file along with your video clip." Step three — render to timeline:
   "download the render and drop it straight into your editing
   timeline." (Step one is PARKED — see header. The shape is the

3. **The effect (visible spec):** body tracking — "this prompt tracks
   your body on every frame so it stays glued to you at all times."
   The X-ray visual rides the body; it does not float free of it.
   Render-to-timeline handoff: the render drops straight into the

4. **No timeline, no editor:** "just one prompt" — no NLE, no human

## Human-made check
"Flat, lifeless and looks amateur" is the tell for a short prompt —
if the effect looks generic, the prompt was too short. The check is
on the render: does the device stay glued to the body on every frame,
or does it drift? Drift means the tracking failed, and the fix is in
the prompt, not in nudging frames by hand.
