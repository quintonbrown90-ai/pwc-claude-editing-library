## The rule
An 18-second video that is one designed graphic + one slowly moving element.
The poster does the selling, the micro-motion holds the eye. A poster with
zero motion is a photo, not a reel — every poster-format video needs exactly
one moving element, and its direction must argue the point.

## Executable steps (rebuild)
1. Poster: one designed graphic, full frame — kicker top-left (the rumour),
   headline (the claim), fix line on a single accent block (the only colour
   on the poster — the eye goes there first), bottom pill + arrow (the
   second hook line that reframes the blame).
2. The moving element: ONE element moves, slowly, the whole video — the
   slider knob drifting left across the track. The drift direction carries
   the argument (away from MAX SLOP, toward NOT SLOP). The motion IS the
   visual argument.
3. Slider-as-argument device: a labeled slider with two poles (e.g. NOT SLOP
   → MAX SLOP; free vs deal-ready; guessing vs system). The knob's position
   IS the claim; its drift IS the fix. Any two-pole spectrum Q wants to
   argue becomes a slider.
4. The curiosity gap IS the funnel: the answer ("the one simple change") is
   never shown on screen — it lives behind the keyword CTA. The poster
   promises, the DM delivers.
