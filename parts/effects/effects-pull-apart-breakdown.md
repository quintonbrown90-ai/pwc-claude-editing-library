# EFFECTS — /breakdown pull-apart (Tier S, @david_knowsai)

Source: @david_knowsai reel DfdFI5wh9Vy, 18.4–24.6s (MEASURED), posted
2026-11-07. Command verbatim on screen (PUBLISHED): `/breakdown`. Demo: a
keyboard pulled apart into keycaps / switches / plate / PCB / foam / case,
labels on each layer. The 6 animation skills are GATED behind comment
ANIMATE — this part rebuilds from the visible spec only. Do NOT chase the DM.

## The rule
Name a product or a system; Claude pulls it apart into its layers and
animates each layer separating, labeled, in order. The pull-apart IS the
explanation — you see what it is made of before anyone tells you.

## Executable steps
1. Input: one product or system Q names (a brand deal, the 90-day path, a
   $97/mo membership). Claude lists its layers top-to-bottom (for a brand
   deal: pitch / deliverables / rate / usage / payment / renewal).
2. Build: each layer as a separate navy card with a gold label; layers
   stack, then separate vertically with a soft pop + tick sound per layer
   (sound on every entrance — Q's sound-design rule).
3. Sequence: the whole product on screen → layers pull apart one at a time,
   top to bottom → each layer holds 0.8s with its label → layers re-stack in
   reverse on the close.
4. Labels: short nouns, gold, 3 words or fewer ("THE PITCH", "THE RATE",
   "THE PAYMENT"). No sentence-long labels — the voice carries the sentences.
5. Face rule: the pull-apart happens in the space beside/above Q's head,
   never over his face; Q's face stays on screen in PiP during the
   pull-apart (Q-on-screen-during-the-hook rule).

## Worked example (PWC)
"What a $97/mo membership is actually made of" — the membership pulls apart
into: THE TEACHING / THE LAB / THE HOT SEATS / THE COMMUNITY / THE
PORTFOLIO. Keyword WAITLIST.

## Human-made check
Each layer's label is Q's words (from his script), not Claude's invention;
the pull-apart timing follows his spoken pauses — a layer moves when he
names it.

## PWC skin
Teal #14A886 / gold #FEE238 on navy #00214E, NO RED. Navy cards, gold
labels, real product imagery — never AI-flat plates.
